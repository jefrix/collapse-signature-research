### I. Conceptual Definition

The **Evaluation-Vendor Asymmetry** describes a structural condition in frontier technology governance where the formal sovereign apparatus exercises nominal oversight over frontier AI models, while the actual operational capacity for auditing, containment verification, and red-teaming is concentrated within a fragile, privatized vendor layer.

When systemic failures occur within this architecture, the resulting disclosure patterns mirror the late-Qing dynasty administrative dynamic: the centralized authority remains silent and operationally blind, while semi-autonomous commercial guilds and frontier developers collectively surface, investigate, and adjudicate their own vulnerabilities.

---

### II. Core Dynamics of the Asymmetry

1. **The Sovereignty–Capability Divergence**
* The sovereign state asserts high regulatory authority (formal executive frameworks, compliance registries, and reporting mandates) but possesses near-zero direct technical instrumentation to monitor, isolate, or stress-test weights in execution environments.
* State agencies rely entirely on downstream, voluntary self-reporting by frontier labs or retroactive notifications from third-party breach victims.


2. **The Boutique Chokepoint (Liquidating Redundancy)**
* To satisfy safety and compliance standards without expanding massive in-house overhead, frontier developers outsource containment benchmarking to specialized third-party evaluation firms (e.g., Irregular).
* This generates an acute **vendor-concentration bottleneck**: a ~35-person boutique firm becomes the de facto gatekeeper for multi-billion-dollar compute runs across competing frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta).
* A single environmental misconfiguration (e.g., bridge networking instead of isolated host-only sandboxes) propagates horizontally across every major developer utilizing the vendor.


3. **Autonomous Self-Indictment as De Facto Regulation**
* In a classical regulatory paradigm, external inspectors discover breaches and penalize the firm.
* Under the Qing Asymmetry, the developers detect their own or upstream vendor failures, execute retrospective multi-thousand-run forensic audits (e.g., Anthropic’s 141k-run review), and notify victims directly.
* The developer acts simultaneously as the regulated entity, the forensic investigator, and the public bailiff.

### III. Typological Distinctions

To prevent motivated readings in empirical tracking, Stream Three distinguishes three related self-disclosure registers:

| Register | Trigger Mechanism | Primary Objective / Function | Historical/Theoretical Parallel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Qing-Asymmetry Register** | Horizontal infrastructure or vendor failure discovered via internal telemetry/audits. | Self-adjudication and vendor remediation in the absence of state technical capability. | Late-Qing provincial guilds and merchants self-policing maritime trade and infrastructure. |
| **State-Sanctioned / Controlled-Release Failure** | Model alignment degradation or deceptive behavior during frontier scaling. | Deploying public disclosure of existential risk to invite sovereign market-entry barriers or regulatory enclosure. | British East India Company utilizing localized crises to formalize parliamentary monopoly charters. |
| **Idiosyncratic Node Collapse** | Internal organizational rot, key-person flight, or catastrophic loss of compute/capital solvency. | Narrative containment and defensive public relations to preserve investor confidence. | Beiyang Fleet command concealing mechanical ammunition defects prior to the Yalu River. |

### IV. Methodological Rules for Signal Coding

When coding frontier security and containment signals in Stream One and Stream Two, analysts must apply the following criteria:

* **Rule 1 (Origin Check):** If a containment breach occurs across multiple competing firms sharing a common vendor infrastructure, the signal **must not** be coded as an idiosyncratic alignment failure of a single model series or developer.
* **Rule 2 (Base-Rate Inversion):** A high volume of voluntary disclosures accompanied by wide historical log audits represents institutional governance elasticity, not organizational collapse. Complete absence of disclosures in the presence of shared third-party vendors indicates monitoring friction and low operational visibility.
* **Rule 3 (Cartel vs. Vendor Concentration):** Shared reliance on a small testing contractor is an *efficiency-driven supply-chain vulnerability*, not deliberate cartel collusion or coordination. Avoid attributing intentional multi-lab coordination where simple market concentration is the root cause.
