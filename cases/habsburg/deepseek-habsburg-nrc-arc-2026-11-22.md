## 7.1 Dynamic NRC Arc (v3.2 required form)
NRC is scored as a trajectory, not a static case value. The three components are
scored at initial, intermediate, and terminal phases.

### Initial NRC (1867–1914): Moderate-to-high
- Regime control of the public sphere: Moderate-to-high. The Dual Monarchy
  managed a complex public sphere through bureaucratic administration,
  censorship where necessary, and dynastic ritual. Nationalist public spheres
  existed but were not yet hegemonic.
- Alternative legitimacy reservoir: High. The dynasty, the Catholic Church, the
  army, and the supranational civil service provided multiple legitimacy
  reservoirs that could be drawn upon if the rising narrative failed.
- External stabilization: High. The alliance with Germany and the broader
  European balance of power provided external security and economic
  stabilization.
Initial NRC: Moderate-to-high.

### Intermediate NRC (1914–1917): Low-to-moderate
- Regime control of the public sphere: Low-to-moderate. Wartime censorship and
  military administration suppressed some public discourse, but the regime
  could not control the proliferation of nationalist and socialist narratives.
  The foreign ministry and military command produced competing narratives.
- Alternative legitimacy reservoir: Moderate. The dynasty’s legitimacy eroded;
  the church and army remained reservoirs, but they were increasingly
  nationalized. The supranational civil service lost credibility as a
  legitimacy source.
- External stabilization: Low-to-moderate. German support continued, but it
  came with dependency and defeat risk. The wider balance of power turned
  against the Central Powers.
Intermediate NRC: Low-to-moderate.

### Terminal NRC (1917–1918): Low
- Regime control of the public sphere: Low. The regime could not control the
  public sphere; nationalist councils, soldiers’ councils, and socialist
  movements produced competing narratives. Censorship collapsed.
- Alternative legitimacy reservoir: Low. The dynasty’s alternative reservoirs
  were exhausted or nationalized. The Emperor’s manifesto of October 1918
  attempted to federalize the empire, but it was too late to constitute an
  alternative legitimacy reservoir.
- External stabilization: Absent. Military defeat and the collapse of the
  German ally removed external stabilization.
Terminal NRC: Low.

### NRC Trajectory: Moderate-to-high → Low-to-moderate → Low
Pattern: Failed Retraction. The regime could not voluntarily withdraw the
rising/continuity narrative without accelerating terminal collapse. The
stability theater of dynastic continuity persisted into 1917 even as structural
recovery capacity fell to zero. The terminal NRC collapse was rapid once the
public sphere and external stabilization failed together.

### Interaction with Stability Theater
The Habsburg case is an anchor for stability theater in v3.2: rituals and
narratives of supranational dynastic continuity persisted through 1917 even
when structural recovery capacity had fallen to zero. Stability theater
sustained the NRC continuity claim until the terminal phase, when military
defeat and nationalist defection made the theater unsustainable.

### Interaction with Self-Indictment Register
The Habsburg elite-literary-terminal register (absurdist and apocalyptic
indictment of the institution as structurally irredeemable) appears in the
0–4 year lead-time range. This is distinct from the non-state-with-state-silence
register and should be coded separately. The terminal NRC collapse and the
elite-literary-terminal indictment co-occur in the final phase, but the causal
direction remains unresolved.
